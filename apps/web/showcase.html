01 · The problem

Credit-risk engineering for people with limited formal history.

MicroScore began with a local question in Pavlodar, Kazakhstan: can a useful decision-support system surface repayment context without pretending that a model should make the final decision?

02 · Research changed the product

The most valuable result was a limitation.

An ablation test showed that the apparent strength of the synthetic model depended heavily on a late-payment proxy. That finding changed the architecture from automation to review support.

04 · Monte Carlo layer

Uncertainty belongs at portfolio level.

Seeded simulations compare policy outcomes under explicit shocks. They do not modify borrower scores and they are presented as scenario planning, not validated loss forecasts.

06 · Responsible-use boundary

A prototype can be ambitious and still be honest.

MicroScore demonstrates the engineering of a review system. It does not claim that synthetic Pavlodar data proves real repayment prediction or that the prototype is ready to process real borrower data.

Not claimedAutomated credit decisions

Every final decision remains with a trained human reviewer.

Not claimedLocal predictive validity

Real regional validation requires consented data and expert partnership.

Not claimedProduction readiness

Identity, transactional delivery, hosting, and privacy review remain pre-pilot gates.

Next responsible milestone Privacy-safe validation with local experts or an MFI partner.

Presenter guide 01 · Frame the problem

Core argument

This is a decision-system project, not a model leaderboard.

Explain the thin-file problem, the Pavlodar context, and the synthetic-data boundary in the same breath. The boundary is part of the engineering result.

Expect
Why is this Computer Engineering?
Answer
It joins data pipelines, ML, typed APIs, persistence, security controls, frontend workflows, and reproducible release evidence.
Evidence · research questions and scope →

Core argument

The ablation result is more important than the headline score.

Define ROC-AUC as ranking quality across thresholds. Then show that removing late_payment_count moves Random Forest from 0.830 to 0.492—near random ranking.

Expect
Why Logistic Regression if Random Forest scores higher?
Answer
Logistic Regression is the operational explanation baseline because contributions are additive and inspectable; neither model is treated as causal.
Evidence · ablation and benchmark results →

Core argument

A probability becomes useful only inside a reviewable workflow.

Trace one application through intake, score provenance, local explanation, affordability checks, human decision, and append-only history.

Expect
How do frontend, API, and database fit together?
Answer
The browser calls typed FastAPI contracts; repositories persist application, score, decision, model, simulation, and audit records with tenant scope.
Evidence · open the analyst workflow →

Core argument

Monte Carlo tests policy uncertainty, not individual borrowers.

Describe the fixed seed, shared macro shock, application-level residual shocks, and paired baseline/adverse/severe scenarios. Repeating inputs and seed reproduces the result.

Expect
Is this a loss forecast or regulatory VaR?
Answer
No. Inputs are scenario assumptions and monetary outputs are prototype units. The layer compares policies and exposes downside uncertainty.

Core argument

The system preserves provenance and constrains unsafe state changes.

Connect versioned models to immutable score packets, terminal decision guards, tenant-scoped repositories, secret-safe audit events, and release checks.

Answer
The 52-method adapter and migration contract are implemented, but managed hosting, identity, email delivery, privacy review, and real-data operations remain gates.

Core argument

Honest non-claims are part of responsible engineering.

State clearly that public behavior is demonstrated, local predictive validity is not, and production authorization is blocked until external evidence exists.

Answer
Consented local data, temporal and external validation, calibration, privacy and legal review, appeal paths, monitoring, and accountable institutional ownership.
